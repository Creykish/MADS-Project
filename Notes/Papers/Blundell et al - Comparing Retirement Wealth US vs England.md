## Reference

Blundell, R., Crawford, R., French, E., & Tetlow, G. (2015). Comparing retirement wealth trajectories on both sides of the pond. *Michigan Retirement Research Center Working Paper*, WP 2015-333.

## Surprising Result

**At age 70-74 in 2002:**
- Median wealth: US $221k, England $228k (similar)
- Life expectancy: similar
- Income: England slightly higher and rising, US falling

**Prediction:** England should run down assets faster (higher income, free healthcare, less need to save)

**Reality:** England ran down slower. Median actually increased 2002-2011.

## The Housing Story

**Housing share of wealth:**
- US: 33% of gross wealth
- England: 67% of gross wealth

**Housing debt:**
- US age 70+: 20% have mortgages
- England age 70+: 5% have mortgages

**House prices 2002-2011:**
- England: strong growth
- US: weak/negative growth

England's wealth increase = house price appreciation. Even non-housing wealth declined slower than US.

## Asset Composition Differences

**England vs US (age 70-74, 2002):**

Prevalence:
- Stocks: England 37%, US 33%
- Bonds: England 40%, US 10% (premium bonds)
- Other property: England 10%, US 17%
- Business assets: England 3%, US 7%

Share of wealth:
- Primary housing: England 66%, US 31%
- Pension assets: England 0%, US 15% (annuitization rules)

Mean housing wealth: England $225k, US $167k (despite lower total wealth!)

## Key Insight

Housing illiquidity matters. People don't/can't draw down housing wealth easily.

England held more wealth in housing → drew down slower overall.

Stripping out house price changes: still slower non-housing drawdown in England.

## Why This Matters for NZ

**NZ similar to England:**
- High home ownership rates (~70% age 65+)
- Housing = large share of wealth
- Strong house price growth (historically)

**Implications:**
- Can't assume retirees will tap housing equity
- Total wealth may not be "available" for spending
- Housing illiquidity = de facto forced savings

**For my model:**
- Should housing be in portfolio optimization?
- Or treat as separate, illiquid asset?
- Affects how much financial wealth needed

**Anarkulova tension:**
- All-equity optimal for financial portfolio
- But if housing = 60-70% of wealth, already have huge illiquid "safe" asset?
- Maybe that justifies even more equity in liquid portfolio?
