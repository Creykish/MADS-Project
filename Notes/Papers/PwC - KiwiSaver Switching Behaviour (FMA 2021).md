## Reference

PwC. (2021, June). *KiwiSaver switching behaviour* (prepared for the Financial Markets Authority). https://www.fma.govt.nz/assets/Reports/KiwiSaver-switching-behaviour-150621.pdf

## Scope and Method

- Commissioned by the FMA after the Feb-Apr 2020 Covid market shock.
- Provider data covered about 2.4 million members total, but comparable switching analysis used about 1.5 million members across 7 providers.
- Main comparison window: Feb-Apr 2020 vs same period in 2019.
- Behavioural lens used to interpret patterns (action bias, salience, herd behavior, present bias, regret aversion, status quo bias, mental accounting).

## Key Results

### Overall switching surge
- 58,356 members in sample (3.9%) switched during Feb-Apr 2020.
- Total switch transactions: 88,112, which is 3.4x the same period in 2019.
- March 2020 switching volume was about 6.4x March 2019.
- Switching remained elevated May-Aug 2020 (about 33.3% above same period in 2019).

### Direction of switching
- 70.5% of identified-direction switches during Covid period were to lower-risk funds.
- In 2019 comparison period, only 27.0% were to lower-risk funds.
- Only 9.1% of lower-risk switchers had switched back to higher risk by Aug 2020.

### Age pattern
- 26-35 year olds made 30.8% of all switches while holding 23.0% of accounts.
- For 26-35 year olds, lower-risk switching was 21x higher than in 2019.
- 75.4% of switches by this group were to lower risk during Covid period (vs 16.4% in 2019).

### Provider/advice pattern
- For 26-35 year olds, lower-risk switches were 26.1x higher for bank customers vs 6.2x for non-bank customers.
- Lower-risk switches were much higher for those without advisers than those with advisers.

## Behavioural interpretation in the report

- Action bias: investors act during uncertainty to feel control.
- Salience + media/social chatter: falling balances become highly attention-grabbing.
- Herd behavior/social norms: people follow what others appear to be doing.
- Present bias: short-term loss avoidance crowds out long-term retirement objective.
- Regret aversion + status quo: once switched, many do not switch back.

Overall:

- Core NZ evidence that volatility shocks trigger behavior that can lock in losses.
- Strong support for [[Volatility Shocks Trigger Behavioral Mistakes in Equity Investors]].
- Supports the implementation caveat in [[All-equity Portfolios Outperform Glidepaths at Long Horizons]]: strategy quality depends on staying invested through turbulence.
