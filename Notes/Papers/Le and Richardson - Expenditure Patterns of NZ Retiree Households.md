## Reference

Le, T., & Richardson, E. (2023). *Expenditure patterns of New Zealand retiree households*. Motu Working Paper 23-07. Motu Economic and Public Policy Research.

## Key Numbers - NZ Retiree Spending

**By age (mean total expenditure):**
- 65-74: $64,700
- 75-84: $46,600 (28% lower)
- 85+: $36,400 (44% lower)

**Composition (2018/19):**
- Groceries: 13%
- Housing: 19%
- Other necessities: 14%
- **Discretionary: 54%** (this is what declines)

**Discretionary drops sharply:**
- Recreation: $8,960 (65-74) → $2,585 (85+) = 71% lower
- Air transport: $3,359 → $801 = 76% lower
- Transport: $5,980 → $2,680 = 55% lower

Necessities stay roughly flat.

## Housing Tenure Matters

**Ownership:**
- 66% own outright
- 14% have mortgage
- 14% rent

**Spending:**
- Outright owners: $55,800 total (highest wellbeing)
- Rent payers: $41,400 (but pay $13,124 in rent, lowest wellbeing)

Home ownership = huge for retirement.

## Household Composition

- Singles: $30,700/year
- Couples: $65,100/year ($32,550 per capita)

Couples have economies of scale but choose higher living standard.

## Wellbeing ≠ Spending

Older retirees report similar/better wellbeing despite 30-44% lower spending.

Suggests utility should focus on discretionary consumption, not total.

**Lowest wellbeing:** rent payers, singles living with others, Māori.

## Why Spending Falls

1. Work expenses cease (transport, clothing)
2. Home production increases (time to cook)
3. Geographic arbitrage (Auckland → provinces)
4. Age discounts (SuperGold)
5. Reduced mobility/preferences

**No sharp drop at 65 in NZ** (unlike US) - NZ Super provides smooth transition.
