## Reference

De Nardi, M., French, E., & Jones, J. B. (2016). Savings after retirement: A survey. *Annual Review of Economics*, 8, 177-204.

## The Puzzle

Retirees, especially rich ones, don't spend down assets as fast as simple life-cycle model predicts.

Over 1/3 of US wealth held by 65+ households.

## Two Groups of Explanations

**Group 1: Risks**
- Uncertain lifespan
- Uncertain medical/LTC expenses
- These create precautionary savings

**Group 2: Bequests**
- Want to leave money to kids
- Reward caregivers
- Exchange motives

**Problem:** Hard to distinguish - both predict slow drawdown.

## Key Facts (AHEAD data, singles)

**Assets by permanent income quintile (age 74):**
- Bottom: ~$0
- Top: ~$200k
- Rich hold onto wealth into 90s

**Mortality bias is huge:**
- Unbalanced panel (including dead): $60k → $44k (age 74-84) = $16k decline
- Balanced panel (survivors only): $84k → $44k = **$40k decline**
- Failure to account for differential mortality understates decline by 150%!

**Medical expenses (mean out-of-pocket):**
- Age 75: <$1k, little income variation
- Age 100: $2.9k (bottom quintile) to $38k (top quintile)
- **Medical expenses are luxury good** - rich spend way more

**Who pays medical costs:**
- Medicare: dominant at young ages
- Medicaid + out-of-pocket: significant at old ages (nursing homes)
- Private insurance: small role
- Top 5% spenders: 35% of total costs, 49% of OOP costs

**Income vs assets:**
- Bottom quintile: $5k/year income, $0 assets
- Top quintile: $23k/year income, $200k assets

## What Models Explain

**Longevity risk alone:** Some slow-down, but not enough

**Medical expense risk:** Explains significant portion, especially:
- Rising with age
- Rising with income (luxury good effect)
- Creates precautionary buffer

**Bequest motives:** Also explains slow drawdown

**Best fit:** Combination of longevity + medical + bequests

## Why Low Annuity/LTC Insurance Demand?

If purely precautionary, should buy:
- Annuities (eliminate longevity risk)
- LTC insurance (eliminate medical risk)

They don't. Why?

**Possible reasons:**
1. Bequest motive (annuities/insurance reduce bequeathable wealth)
2. Medicaid crowds out (if qualify for Medicaid, don't need insurance)
3. Adverse selection / loading factors
4. Lack of inflation protection

## Couples vs Singles

Singles decumulate faster than couples (Hurd 1990 finding).

Possible reasons:
- Couples live longer (joint mortality)
- Stronger bequest motive to children
- Economies of scale → less need to drawdown

(Section 7 of paper discusses couples in detail)

## Policy Implications

Estate taxation:
- No bequest motive → tax doesn't distort (just transfer)
- Strong bequest → tax distorts savings (GDP cost ~0.7%, Cagetti & DeNardi)

Medicaid asset testing:
- Creates implicit 100% tax on savings for medical events
- Discourages saving by low-income
