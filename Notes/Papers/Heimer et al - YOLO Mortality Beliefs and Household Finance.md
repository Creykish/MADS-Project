## Reference

Heimer, R. Z., Myrseth, K. O. R., & Schoenle, R. S. (2015). YOLO: Mortality beliefs and household finance puzzles. *Brandeis University Working Paper*, 97.

## The Finding

People are terrible at estimating their own mortality:

**Young people (age 28):**
- True survival to 33: 99.4%
- Believed survival: 92.8%
- **Underestimate by 7ppt**

**Old people (age 68):**
- True survival to 78: 71.4%
- Believed survival: 82.4%
- **Overestimate by 11ppt**

Pattern: Young = too pessimistic about short-term survival. Old = too optimistic about long-term survival.

## Why It Happens

**Salience / availability heuristic:**

Young people think about:
- Plane crashes, car accidents, rare events
- Overweight these low-probability deaths

Old people think about:
- Health, normal aging process
- Compare themselves to already-dead cohort members → "I'm healthier than average" (selection bias)

## Financial Consequences

**Survey evidence:**

Large belief errors correlate with:
- Not saving month-to-month
- Over-reliance on credit cards
- Lower financial literacy
- Less investment experience
- Belief they'll use savings faster

**Life-cycle model results:**

Embedding biased beliefs:
- Young undersave by **10%** at retirement (too pessimistic → don't save enough)
- Retirees consume **12%** less (too optimistic → save too much)

## Equity Premium Implication

With subjective mortality beliefs, required equity premium increases **3x** even with risk aversion γ=2.

Why? People overestimate extreme consumption declines (death) → demand higher returns to hold risky assets.

Partial equilibrium rare-event story (like Rietz, Barro).

## Method

400 respondents, ages 28/38/48/58/68, asked:
- Survival probability at 1, 2, 5, 10 year horizons
- Compare to SSA actuarial tables

Robust to:
- Providing actuarial data beforehand (doesn't help!)
- Numerical literacy (errors smaller but still there)
- Gender (similar patterns)

## For My Research

**Good news:** NZ Super might partially correct young people's undersaving (provides floor regardless of beliefs).

**Bad news:** If NZ retirees also overestimate longevity, they'll overconsume early → poor outcomes if they actually do live long.

**Questions:**
1. Do NZ mortality beliefs match US patterns?
2. Does higher NZ Super floor reduce impact of biased beliefs?
3. Should optimal advice account for belief biases or try to correct them?
