The state of having accumulated enough wealth and assets that generate sufficient passive income to cover one's living expenses for the rest of their life, without needing to rely on active employment or financial assistance from others, including via [[Pensions & Social Security]].

## Why It Matters for Lifecycle Modelling

- [[Destitution Risk]] is the failure mode: running out of money -> forced back onto minimum income
- Financial independence is the goal: never needing to work again and never running out
- [[The Life Cycle Model of Consumption]] predicts retirees decumulate to zero - in practice, most NZ retirees maintain or grow financial independence due to NZ Super (see [[Retirees Spend Less in Retirement than Predicted by Theory]])

## The FIRE Number

A common heuristic: financial independence requires **25x annual spending** invested in stocks/bonds (the "4% rule"):
- Withdraw 4% of portfolio per year -> portfolio should survive 30 years
- Driven by historical US data (Bengen 1994, Trinity Study)
- **With NZ Super, the required FIRE number is much smaller:**
  - No Frills provincial single: annual spending ~$30,400 (2025 RIIG)
  - NZ Super covers ~$28,900 -> only ~$1,500/year drawdown needed
  - Required capital for drawdown: $37,500 (at 4% rule)
  - -> essentially, NZ Super alone provides financial independence for provincial No Frills retirees

## Savings Cost of Portfolio Choice

([[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]] 2025)
- The portfolio decision has enormous impact on how much savings are needed
- A TDF investor needs **63% more savings** than an all-equity investor to achieve the same utility
- A 60/40 investor needs **94% more savings**
- -> poor portfolio choice dramatically raises the savings rate required for financial independence

## NZ Super as the Floor

- NZ Super provides a universal income floor that partially fulfils financial independence for all NZ 65+ residents
- This unique feature means the "independence" threshold is only the gap above NZ Super spending
- Couple: NZ Super ~$41,557/year after tax (2025 rates); RIIG No Frills couple ~$46,600/year -> very small gap
