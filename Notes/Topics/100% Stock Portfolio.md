A [[Life-cycle Asset Allocation]] strategy that allocates 100% of financial assets to equities (stocks) throughout the investor's life, with no bonds or bills.

## The Case For

**Long-horizon mean reversion** ([[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]] 2025):
- With [[Block Bootstrap Returns]], stock variance ratio = **0.75** at 30-year horizon (variance shrinks)
- Bond variance ratio = **2.30** at 30 years (variance grows)
- Stocks are a better inflation hedge: corr(stocks, inflation) = -0.01 vs corr(bonds, inflation) = -0.78
- All-equity has **lower ruin probability** (6.7%) vs TDF (19.7%) vs 60/40 (16.9%)

**Welfare cost comparison:**
- 60/40 balanced: requires 94% more savings to match all-equity utility
- TDF: requires 63% more savings

**[[Declining-risk Strategies are sub-optimal]] evidence:**
- [[Estrada - The Glidepath Illusion]] (2014): 100% equity outperforms glidepaths in 19 countries
- [[Dolvin - Asset Allocation for Retirement Simple Heuristics and Target-Date Funds]] (2010): bootstrapped simulations confirm 100% stocks beats [[Age in Bonds Rule]] variants
- [[Shiller - Life-cycle personal accounts proposal for Social Security]] (2006): 100% equity **stochastically dominates** baseline lifecycle (85->15%) at every percentile; median $157k vs $15k; loss prob 2% vs 32%; result survives international return adjustment (4.8% stocks instead of 6.8%)

## Optimal Composition (Anarkulova 2025)

Not just any stocks:
- **33% domestic equities + 67% international equities**
- Reason: international diversification provides better long-run diversification than domestic bonds
- In small markets like NZ (0.1% of global market cap), this argues for very high international weight
- International stocks: lower long-run correlation with domestic than bonds have

## NZ Context

- [[Pensions & Social Security]]: NZ Super PV ~$800k per single = large implicit bond-like asset
- Already heavily "safe" via NZ Super -> financial portfolio should lean equity
- [[Consumption Floor]]: NZ floor is high -> downside bounded -> more tolerance for volatility
- [[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]]: explicitly notes NZ Super as justification for higher equity

## Qualifications and Exceptions

**[[Parker et al - Simple Allocation Rules and Optimal Portfolio Choice]] (2024):**
- Heterogeneity matters: 90th pct household -> ~100% equity; 10th pct -> <20% equity in retirement
- Wealthier households can tolerate more equity (hit consumption floor doesn't matter much)
- Poorer households near the floor need capital preservation
- Business cycle state: less equity in recessions when expected returns are lower relative to uncertainty

**[[Return Predictability]]:**
- When dividend-price ratio is low -> expected returns are low -> case for equity weaker
- Conversely high yield -> buy more equities

**Sequence of returns risk:**
- 100% equity exposes retiree to bad early returns that permanently deplete capital
- Mitigated by NZ Super floor and by block-bootstrap mean reversion
- See [[Destitution Risk]]
