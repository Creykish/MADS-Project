Riskier financial assets are associated with higher expected returns. This is the foundational principle behind holding equities over bonds.

## The Equity Premium

- Equities have returned ~6-7% per year real (after inflation) over long US history
- Bonds: ~1-2% per year real
- Cash/bills: ~0% real
- The equity risk premium (ERP) = excess return of stocks over risk-free = approximately 4-6% historically

**[[Risk-Return Trade Off Long Run]] Lundblad (2007):**
- Using US data 1836-2003, finds a positive and statistically significant risk-return tradeoff in equities
- Holds even over very long samples; not a fluke of the post-WW2 period
- Key point: the relationship is weak at short horizons but significant at long horizons

## Horizon Dependence (Variance Ratios)

([[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]] 2025)

Variance ratios tell you whether volatility scales faster or slower than IID implies:
- **Variance ratio < 1** -> mean reversion (safer than short-run stats suggest)
- **Variance ratio > 1** -> momentum/persistence (more dangerous than short-run stats suggest)

| Asset | Variance ratio at 30 years |
|---|---|
| Domestic stocks | 0.75 |
| International stocks | lower than bonds |
| Bonds | 2.30 |
| Bills | approaches 2.0 |

**Implication:** Standard portfolio construction using 1-month statistics overstates the long-run benefit of bonds. For a 30-year investor, stocks are the lower-variance asset.

## Bond/Inflation Correlation

- Bonds: corr(returns, inflation) = **-0.78** -> bonds get crushed when inflation rises
- Stocks: corr(returns, inflation) = **-0.01** -> equities are approximately inflation-neutral in the long run

This is critical for retirees whose main risk is inflation eroding spending power: bonds offer negative inflation protection at long horizons.

## [[Return Predictability]]

Expected equity returns are not constant:
- High dividend-price ratio -> higher expected future returns
- Business cycle state (expansion vs recession) affects expected returns
- -> optimal [[Life-cycle Asset Allocation]] should vary with these state variables, not just age
