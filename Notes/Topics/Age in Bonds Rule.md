A [[Life-cycle Asset Allocation]] method that linearly increases an investors exposure to bonds as a function of their age. A type of [[Decreasing Equity Strategy]].

## Rule Variants

- **Classic:** % bonds = age (age 60 -> 60% bonds, 40% stocks)
- **Modern variant:** % stocks = 120 - age (age 60 -> 60% stocks, 40% bonds)
- **Aggressive variant:** % stocks = 140 - age (age 60 -> 80% stocks)

[[Dolvin - Asset Allocation for Retirement Simple Heuristics and Target-Date Funds]] (2010): most [[Target Date Funds]] in practice approximate the 120-minus-age rule.

## Origin and Rationale

Simple heuristic derived from the [[Human Capital]] argument:
- Young -> large human capital (bond-like) -> financial portfolio should be equity heavy
- Old -> human capital depleted -> financial portfolio tilts to bonds
- Rule linearly approximates this shift without requiring any calculation

## Problems

1. **Ignores wealth level** - a 60-year-old with $10M has different needs than one with $100k
2. **Ignores NZ Super** ([[Pensions & Social Security]]) - already provides a large bond-like income floor, so financial portfolio doesn't need extra bonds
3. **Ignores return predictability** ([[Return Predictability]]) - optimal allocation varies with market conditions
4. **Bonds fail at long horizons** ([[Block Bootstrap Returns]]): variance ratio 2.30 at 30 years vs stocks 0.75
5. **Linear** - no reason the relationship should be strictly linear

## Empirical Performance

All evidence points to the Age in Bonds Rule producing inferior outcomes:
- [[Estrada - The Glidepath Illusion]]: increasing equity outperforms in 19/19 countries
- [[Dolvin - Asset Allocation for Retirement Simple Heuristics and Target-Date Funds]]: [[100% Stock Portfolio]] beats all age-in-bonds variants
- [[Shiller - Life-cycle personal accounts proposal for Social Security]]: proposed age-in-bonds scheme for Social Security underperforms
- [[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]]: 60/40 (milder version of same idea) requires 94% more savings
