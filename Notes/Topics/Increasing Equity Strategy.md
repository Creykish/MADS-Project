A [[Life-cycle Asset Allocation]] strategy that increases equity exposure with age. The opposite of a [[Decreasing Equity Strategy]]. Also known as a "rising glidepath" or "V-shaped" allocation.

## The Core Argument

If stocks mean-revert at long horizons, then holding more stocks in retirement (when you're drawing down) may be less risky than holding them during accumulation. The logic:
- Young investor: high equity -> single bad year = small absolute loss (small portfolio)
- Old investor starting retirement: high equity -> single bad year hits harder BUT stocks have already mean-reverted by now

Alternatively: in early retirement, bond-heavy allocation helps avoid sequence-of-returns risk. As the danger window passes, equity can increase.

## Empirical Evidence

**[[Estrada - The Glidepath Illusion]] (2014):**
- Tested [[Life-cycle Asset Allocation]] strategies across 19 countries with historical data
- Increasing equity outperformed decreasing equity in all 19 countries
- The "illusion" is that declining risk looks prudent but produces inferior outcomes
- Difference in terminal wealth is large: increasing equity -> significantly more wealth at death

**[[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]] (2025):**
- [[Block Bootstrap Returns]] show stocks mean-revert at 30-year horizon (variance ratio = 0.75)
- This is the key mechanism: stocks are actually safer at the long horizons retirees face
- Optimal strategy = constant 100% equity (not strictly increasing, but non-declining)

## Rising Glidepath Variant

A specific version proposed in some literature: start retirement conservative (say 30% equity) and increase equity as retirement progresses. Rationale:
- Avoids sequence-of-returns risk in the vulnerable early years (sequence risk is highest when portfolio is largest)
- After ~10 years, the portfolio is smaller -> absolute dollar risk is lower -> can afford more equity
- NZ context: as NZ Super covers progressively more spending (real spending declines ~2%/year), less capital needed -> can accept more risk with the remainder

## Relationship to Other Strategies

- Strictly more aggressive than [[100% Stock Portfolio]] on the way up if starting low
- Reflects [[Sequence of Returns Risk]] thinking rather than simple human capital logic
- Contrasts with [[Decreasing Equity Strategy]] (conventional wisdom)

## Criticism

- Empirical support depends heavily on historical data and geography
- May not account for investor psychology: many retirees would panic-sell during the equity ramp-up phase
- [[Heimer et al - YOLO Mortality Beliefs and Household Finance]]: biased mortality beliefs mean retirees may not implement a rising glidepath rationally
