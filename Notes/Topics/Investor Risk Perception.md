How investors perceive and respond to investment risk, particularly volatility. Risk perception is not the same as objective volatility: investors use heuristics that can systematically mismatch the mathematical risk of an asset.

## What Drives Risk Perception

**For finance professionals** ([[Huber Huber Kirchler - Volatility Shocks and Investment Behavior]] 2022):
- Volatility per se -> professionals correctly identify a volatility spike as increasing risk, regardless of whether prices moved up, down, or flat
- Investment propensity moves with the *direction* of price changes (not just volatility)
- Risk perception and investment satisfaction are independent psychological dimensions

**For lay investors / students:**
- Risk perception driven primarily by the *frequency of negative returns*, not by variance
- A positive volatility shock (high variance but mostly up moves) is not perceived as risky
- -> lay investors only react to downward volatility, not upside volatility

## The Key Disconnect from Theory

Standard portfolio theory (Merton, Tobin) says investors should reduce holdings proportionally when volatility rises, and restore them when it falls, keeping expected utility constant.

In practice:
- Investors reduce holdings after downward shocks more than theory predicts
- They do not fully restore holdings after volatility subsides (persistent fear effect)
- Even upward shocks cause professionals to increase perceived risk -> reduce equity
- -> systematic under-holding of equities during and after volatile periods

## Consequences for [[Life-cycle Asset Allocation]]

The behavioral gap between optimal and actual allocation is largest during volatility spikes - exactly when equity valuations are often most attractive (post-crash). This creates a double penalty for investors who reduce holdings after volatility:
1. They sell at low prices (performance drag)
2. They miss the recovery (opportunity cost)

Combined: actual outcomes from holding strategies fall below theoretical predictions. This is a significant caveat to the [[100% Stock Portfolio]] recommendation: the strategy is theoretically superior but requires holding through volatility episodes that most investors find psychologically difficult.

## Sequence of Returns Risk as Behavioral Risk

The conventional framing of sequence-of-returns risk is mathematical (early negative returns deplete capital irreversibly). But there is also a behavioral dimension: volatility shocks trigger selling, which converts paper losses into realised losses and breaks the mean-reversion benefit that makes long-run equities safer ([[Block Bootstrap Returns]]).
