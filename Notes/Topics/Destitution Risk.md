The risk that the retiree exhausts their wealth before they die. Sometimes called "Life-Span Risk" or "ruin risk" or "sequence-of-returns risk".

Essentially: the [[Life Cycle Wealth Trajectory]] terminates before the end of the investor's lifespan. Results in the loss of [[Financial Independence]] and worse.

## Sources of Destitution Risk

1. **Sequence of returns risk:** Large negative returns early in retirement permanently deplete capital that would otherwise compound. The same average return produces worse outcomes if losses come early vs late.
2. **Longevity risk:** Living longer than expected exhausts savings planned for a shorter horizon. Retirees systematically overestimate their survival probability late in life ([[Heimer et al - YOLO Mortality Beliefs and Household Finance]])
3. **Medical expense shocks:** Catastrophic health costs ([[Medical Expenses]]) can deplete assets rapidly, especially in the US. NZ context: less severe due to public healthcare but residential care is a risk.
4. **Spending above capacity:** Withdrawing too much too early, especially if spending doesn't decline as expected

## Mitigation

- **[[Pensions & Social Security]] (NZ Super):** Provides a floor. In NZ, destitution risk is partially bounded by NZ Super = $28,900/year single. True destitution (zero income) is not possible while NZ Super exists.
- **Diversification and [[Asset Allocation]]:** A well-constructed portfolio can reduce sequence risk. [[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]]: 100% equity has **lower ruin probability** (6.7%) than TDF (19.7%) or 60/40 (16.9%) - counterintuitive but driven by long-run mean reversion
- **Flexible spending:** Reducing withdrawal when portfolio falls ([[Retirement Consumption Decline]] happens naturally)
- **[[Precautionary Saving]]:** Holding a buffer above expected needs

## NZ-Specific Ruin Probability

With NZ Super as floor, the practical question changes from "will I have zero income?" to "will I have to cut spending below NZ Super level?" This reframes destitution risk as a spending shortfall risk above the floor, not absolute ruin.

For No Frills provincial single: NZ Super alone covers most spending -> financial assets only needed for metro/lifestyle gap -> destitution risk is low for most retirees.
