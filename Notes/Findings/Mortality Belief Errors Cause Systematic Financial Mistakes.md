Biased mortality beliefs cause young people to undersave and old people to underconsume, creating systematic lifecycle errors. These errors compound over time and have significant financial consequences.

## Evidence from Heimer et al (2015)

[[Heimer et al - YOLO Mortality Beliefs and Household Finance]] (Heimer, Myrseth, Schoenle 2015):

**Young people underestimate survival:**
- Age 28 respondents underestimate their 5-year survival probability by **7 percentage points**
- Effect on saving: undersave ~**10% less** at retirement relative to correctly-calibrated individual
- Mechanism: "YOLO" logic - you might die soon so no point saving
- Systematic, not random error

**Old people overestimate survival:**
- Age 68 respondents overestimate their 10-year survival probability by **11 percentage points**
- Effect on consumption: underconsume ~**12% less** than optimal (too cautious about outliving wealth)
- Mechanism: overestimate longevity -> hold back spending "just in case"
- Creates excess precautionary saving and slow decumulation

**Equity premium:**
- With biased mortality beliefs, equity premium required to justify stock-holding increases by **3x**
- Biased beliefs make investors behave as though stocks are far riskier than they are

## Interaction with NZ Super

[[Pensions & Social Security]] (NZ Super) partially corrects the young undersaving problem:
- Even if a young person undersaves by 10%, NZ Super provides a guaranteed floor at 65
- The floor doesn't fix the welfare loss entirely but bounds the downside
- NZ Super helps correct the YOLO undersaving problem at a population level

## Interaction with Retirement Underconsumption

[[Retirees Spend Less in Retirement than Predicted by Theory]]:
- Part of the empirical underconsumption finding may be mortality belief errors, not rational precautionary saving
- Old people holding back because they overestimate remaining life -> look like precautionary savers but are actually poorly calibrated
- Hard to disentangle from genuine precautionary motives

## Implication for Optimal Policy

If retirees systematically overestimate longevity:
- They underconsume and die with more wealth than intended
- The bequest is accidental rather than intended ([[Bequeathment not significant motive]])
- Optimal advice: explicit consumption path targets, annuitization, or drawdown rules to counteract the bias
- NZ Super reduces but does not eliminate the problem (people still underspend above NZ Super)
