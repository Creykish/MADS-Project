The empirically optimal glidepath across the full life cycle is an **inverted U-shape**: equity should increase during the accumulation period and decrease during the retirement/drawdown period, with maximum equity exposure at the point of retirement.

This is the direct opposite of the U-shaped glidepath recommended by Pfau & Kitces (2014), which suggests declining equity during accumulation and rising equity during retirement.

## Evidence

**[[Estrada - The Glidepath Illusion]] (2014) — accumulation period:**
- 19 countries + world, DMS data 1900-2009, 71 overlapping 40-year working lifetimes
- Contrarian (rising equity) beats lifecycle (declining equity) in all 19 countries on mean and median terminal wealth
- 100% equity throughout doubles mean terminal wealth vs average lifecycle strategy
- Downside protection similar or better with contrarian -> the higher SD of contrarian is all upside variance
- Conclusion: equity should **increase** during working years

**[[Estrada - The Retirement Glidepath]] (2015) — retirement period:**
- Same DMS dataset, 81 rolling 30-year retirement periods, 4% withdrawal rule
- Declining-equity (DE) strategies have lower failure rates AND higher bequests than rising-equity (RE) mirrors
- US failure rates: DE 4.9-8.6% vs RE mirrors 8.6-21.0%
- International (avg 19 countries): DE 31-32% vs RE 39-49%
- Conclusion: equity should **decrease** during retirement

Combined: **inverted U** = low equity early career -> builds up -> peak at retirement -> gradually declines during drawdown.

## The Practical Simplification

Both papers also find that a **constant 60/40 allocation** is "very effective" or near-optimal in its respective period:
- During accumulation: 60x40 beats all lifecycle glidepaths, though still well below 100% equity
- During retirement: 60x30 has the lowest failure rate of all strategies (4.9% US) and highest mean bequest

-> The inverted U is theoretically interesting but operationally the 60/40 static is a reasonable approximation and much simpler to implement.

-> For accumulation, 100% equity is even better than 60/40 (Anarkulova 2025, Estrada 2014).

## What Explains the Asymmetry?

**During accumulation:** portfolio is small early -> risk tolerance is high in absolute dollar terms -> be aggressive -> grow the base. Contrarian strategies are "aggressive with a large portfolio, conservative with a small one" which matches when capital is at risk.

**During retirement:** sequence-of-returns risk dominates in early retirement when portfolio is largest -> being equity-heavy early in retirement and transitioning to bonds preserves capital when portfolio shrinks. RE strategies are vulnerable: waiting to increase equity means any equity gains arrive when the portfolio has already been depleted by withdrawals.

## Dissent: Pfau & Kitces (2014)

Pfau & Kitces recommend **U-shape** (declining accumulation, rising retirement) using Monte Carlo simulations. Their argument: sequence risk is worst in early retirement -> start conservative, grow equity as danger window passes. Estrada's historical rolling period analysis contradicts this. The disagreement is methodological: MC with IID returns loses the autocorrelation structure that makes early retirement equity exposure work.
