# NZ Super Covers Most Retiree Spending Needs

For the majority of New Zealand retirees, NZ Super alone covers most — and sometimes all — of normal living expenses. Private savings are needed primarily for a modest top-up and to manage uncertainty, not to fund the bulk of retirement consumption.

## Key Numbers (Matthews 2025, RIIG 2024)

**NZ Super rates (2025, after tax):**
- Single: $27,013/year ($519/week)
- Couple: $41,557/year ($799/week)

**No Frills spending benchmarks (Matthews 2025):**
| Household | Weekly | Annual | NZ Super coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single (Metro) | $706 | $36,680 | 73% |
| Single (Provincial) | $581 | $30,180 | 89% |
| Couple (Metro) | $962 | $49,980 | 83% |
| Couple (Provincial) | $842 | $43,760 | 95% |

For provincial couples, NZ Super is essentially sufficient for No Frills living — only ~$2,200/year in drawdown needed.

## Savings Targets Are Lower Than Assumed

RIIG (2024) savings target comparison for a single retiree assuming 6% real return, spending declining 2%/year in real terms:
- Required pool (No Frills gap coverage only): **~$80–120k** depending on location
- Full "Choices" lifestyle: ~$260–375k

Much lower than the $500k+ figures cited in popular financial planning that assume constant real withdrawals.

## Declining Spending Reinforces Coverage

As spending declines ~2%/year in real terms after 65, NZ Super's coverage *increases* over time:
- At 65: NZ Super may cover 73–89% of spending
- At 80: real spending is ~26% lower; NZ Super (inflation-indexed) now covers 90–100% for most
- At 85+: many provincial retirees may require zero drawdown from financial assets

This means the effective drawdown period is shorter than life expectancy suggests — wealth may only need to fund 10–15 years of gap spending, not 20–30 years of full living expenses.

## Implications

1. **Savings benchmarks based on full income replacement are too high** for the majority of NZ retirees
2. **Portfolio design:** NZ Super acts as a large riskless income floor — the optimal portfolio for the financial portion can afford to be more equity-heavy
3. **Policy:** The universal, non-means-tested nature of NZ Super avoids asset-test kinks that distort savings behaviour in Australia
4. **KiwiSaver:** As it matures, future retirees will have additional savings on top of NZ Super — reducing the importance of the floor effect but increasing the role of investment strategy

## Caveats

- Metro retirees and homeowners (rates, maintenance) face higher costs — NZ Super coverage lower
- "Choices" lifestyle requires significantly more than NZ Super alone
- Housing costs highly variable: renters face acute shortfall — NZ Super only covers ~60% of a renter's No Frills costs
- Rising means-testing of residential care subsidy creates late-life cost uncertainty
