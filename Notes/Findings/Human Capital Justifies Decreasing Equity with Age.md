Young investors with large [[Human Capital]] that behaves like a bond should hold more equities in their financial portfolio, then gradually reduce equity as human capital depletes. This provides the main theoretical justification for [[Decreasing Equity Strategy]] glidepaths.

## The Argument

([[Cocco - Consumption and Portfolio Choice over the Life Cycle]] 2005, [[Merton & Bodie - Labor supply flexibility and portfolio choice in a life cycle model]] 1992)

Total wealth = financial wealth + human capital (PV of future wages)

For a young worker:
- Human capital >> financial wealth
- Human capital is bond-like (stable wages, low correlation with market)
- Total implicit portfolio is already heavily bond-like
- To reach optimal total-portfolio risk: financial portfolio must be heavily equity
- As worker ages -> human capital depletes -> financial portfolio tilts toward bonds

This generates a declining equity glidepath as the natural consequence of optimal total-portfolio management.

## Labor Flexibility Amplifier

([[Merton & Bodie - Labor supply flexibility and portfolio choice in a life cycle model]])
- Workers can adjust hours, extend working years, or retire early
- This labor hedge creates an additional bond-like buffer
- More flexibility -> more equity risk-taking in financial portfolio
- Risky human capital (volatile wages, commission-based) -> reduce financial equity

## The Limits: Bond Quality Matters

The argument assumes bonds are the right asset to substitute for depleting human capital. [[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]] (2025) challenges this:

- At 30-year horizon, bond variance ratio = 2.30 -> bonds get riskier, not safer
- The asset meant to REPLACE human capital (bonds) is actually a poor long-term asset
- International stocks provide better long-run diversification than bonds
- -> the human capital argument for declining equity is sound in theory but the conclusion changes because bonds are suboptimal

**Reconciliation:**
- Young investors SHOULD hold more equity than old investors (human capital argument is valid)
- But old investors should NOT substitute into bonds - they should hold international diversified stocks
- -> the declining equity glidepath concept is right directionally but leads to the wrong destination

## Risky Human Capital Cases

When human capital is risky (correlates with equity):
- Finance worker: wages highly correlated with market -> hold LESS financial equity
- Tech worker: wages volatile, correlated with tech sector -> diversify away
- Civil servant: wages stable, bond-like -> hold MORE financial equity
