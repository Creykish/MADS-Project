At long horizons (30+ years), a [[100% Stock Portfolio]] produces lower ruin probabilities, higher terminal wealth, and better welfare outcomes than any glidepath or balanced portfolio. This is the main finding of several empirical studies and challenges the conventional wisdom behind [[Target Date Funds]].

## Evidence

**[[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]] (2025) - strongest evidence:**
- Using [[Block Bootstrap Returns]] (not IID) with international equities:
- All-equity ruin probability: **6.7%** vs TDF: **19.7%** vs 60/40: **16.9%**
- TDF requires 63% more savings to match all-equity utility
- 60/40 requires 94% more savings to match all-equity utility
- Optimal portfolio: 33% domestic + 67% international stocks, 0% bonds, 0% bills
- Exception: 27% bills at exact age 65 for tactical liquidity (4% rule mechanics)\n
**[[Dolvin - Asset Allocation for Retirement Simple Heuristics and Target-Date Funds]] (2010):**
- Bootstrapped US historical returns
- 100% equities beats all [[Age in Bonds Rule]] variants (120-age, 110-age, 100-age) on terminal wealth\n
**[[Shiller - Life-cycle personal accounts proposal for Social Security]] (2006):**
- Historical US data 1871-2004
- A Social Security personal account invested 100% in equities would likely have outperformed the proposed declining-equity scheme


[[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]]:

-  The reversal happens because of long-horizon return properties ([[Block Bootstrap Returns]]):
- Stocks mean-revert: variance ratio = 0.75 at 30 years -> stocks get relatively safer over time
- Bonds diverge: variance ratio = 2.30 at 30 years -> bonds get riskier over time
- Bond/stock correlation rises from ~0.1 (short) to 0.45 (long) -> diversification shrinks
- Bond/inflation corr = -0.78 -> bonds destroy real value in inflationary environments



The standard short-run statistics (1-month variance) make bonds look safe and stocks look risky. But this reverses completely at 30-year horizons.


## Limitations

- [[Heimer et al - YOLO Mortality Beliefs and Household Finance]]: biased beliefs may prevent investors from holding all-equity during bad periods
- Short-term volatility is real: an all-equity investor faces larger paper losses during downturns (sequence-of-returns risk)
- Volatility may cause behavioral mistakes or forced selling ([[Volatility Shocks Trigger Behavioral Mistakes in Equity Investors]])
- Heterogeneity ([[Optimal Allocation Depends on Wealth and Cycle State]]): very low wealth households may need capital preservation over equity growth
