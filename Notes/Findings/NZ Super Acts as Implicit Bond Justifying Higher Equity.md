New Zealand Superannuation, when treated as part of the retiree's total wealth portfolio, functions as a very large inflation-linked bond-like asset. This has major implications for the optimal allocation of the remaining financial portfolio.

## The Implicit Bond Calculation

NZ Super (2025 rates, single, after tax): ~$28,900/year
- Real discount rate: ~2%
- Expected retirement period: 25 years (65 to 90 at median survival)
- Present value at 2% real discount rate: **~$550k**
- If life expectancy extends: PV can reach $700k-$800k+

This single asset dwarfs the typical NZ retiree's financial portfolio (median KiwiSaver balance at 65 is ~$150k-$250k).

## Implications for Asset Allocation

([[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]] 2025)\n- The total implicit portfolio is already heavily "safe" via NZ Super's bond-like properties\n- Optimal response: financial portfolio should be 100% equity to provide the risk-return balance\n- This is exactly what Anarkulova et al. find for the US Social Security case\n- NZ Super is even more generous relative to typical savings -> argument is even stronger in NZ

## Comparison to the US

In the US, Social Security benefits are approximately $20-25k/year (median). Still a large bond-like asset, but NZ Super's:
- Universal nature (no earnings-test, no means-test) makes it more reliable as a floor
- Inflation indexation (CPI + wage floor) provides better inflation protection than US Social Security COLA
- -> NZ retirees face less idiosyncratic NZ Super risk than US counterparts

## Interaction with [[Human Capital]]

Pre-retirement: human capital (bond-like) -> financial portfolio should be equity heavy ([[Human Capital]])
Post-retirement: NZ Super (bond-like) -> financial portfolio should remain equity heavy
-> the case for holding bonds in the financial portfolio is weak at BOTH stages of the lifecycle for NZ investors

## [[Consumption Floor]] Link

NZ Super nearly covers spending for modest retirees:
- No Frills provincial single: NZ Super covers ~89% of spending
- -> even if financial portfolio drops to zero, the retiree is not destitute
- -> tolerance for financial portfolio volatility is high
