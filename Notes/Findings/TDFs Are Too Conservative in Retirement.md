[[Target Date Funds]] hold too little equity after retirement. The theoretically optimal allocation in retirement is substantially higher than what TDFs provide, implying a meaningful welfare cost from using the conventional product.

## Evidence

**[[Parker et al - Simple Allocation Rules and Optimal Portfolio Choice]] (2024) - primary evidence:**
- RL-solved optimal equity share at retirement: **~60%**
- Typical TDF equity at retirement: **30-40%**
- Discrepancy: TDFs under-weight equity by ~20-30 percentage points post-retirement
- Welfare cost from using TDF instead of optimal:
  - **1.7% of lifetime consumption** (if investor can re-optimize other decisions)
  - **2.8% of lifetime consumption** (if fully locked into TDF)
- These are large numbers: losing 1.7-2.8% of consumption equivalent wealth over a lifetime

**[[Anarkulova et al - Beyond the Status Quo (Critical Assessment of Lifecycle Advice)]] (2025):**
- TDF produces 71% of the terminal wealth of all-equity portfolio in welfare equivalent terms
- TDF ruin probability: 19.7% vs all-equity 6.7%
- Even in retirement, all-equity is safer over the long run due to [[Block Bootstrap Returns]] mean reversion

## Why TDFs Are Too Conservative in Retirement

1. **Long horizon continues:** A 65-year-old with a 25-year expected retirement still faces a long horizon. Bonds deteriorate at long horizons (VR=2.30 at 30 years)
2. **NZ Super as floor:** With [[Pensions & Social Security]] providing guaranteed income, financial portfolio downside is already bounded -> high equity is justified
3. **Spending naturally declines:** [[Retirement Consumption Decline]] means the required drawdown shrinks over time, reducing sequence-of-returns risk
4. **Missed state variables:** TDFs ignore wealth level, business cycle, dividend yield - all of which the optimal policy uses (see [[Optimal Allocation Depends on Wealth and Cycle State]])

## Why TDFs Exist Despite Being Suboptimal

- Behavioral: "safer as you age" is intuitive and reduces anxiety during drawdowns
- Regulatory: QDIA default in US/NZ retirement systems
- Marketing: easy to explain and sell
- Legacy: pre-dates the block bootstrap evidence

## NZ KiwiSaver Implication

KiwiSaver conservative and balanced funds (which dominate NZ retirement savings) replicate TDF logic. Retirees switching from growth to conservative/balanced funds at 65 are:
- Likely dramatically over-allocated to bonds/cash
- Potentially facing higher ruin probability than if they stayed in growth
- Missing returns they could have earned
